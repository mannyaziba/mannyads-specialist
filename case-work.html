Asset 01

Ad Disapproval: Diagnosis & Resolution

Case ADS-002 · Account Suspension — Suspicious Payment Activity

The Problem
Issue
Google Ads account suspended — ad serving stopped completely
Reported
Client noticed ads offline overnight with no warning email
Initial read
Likely policy flag — billing mismatch or landing page issue
Investigation
  • Checked dashboard — status showed "Suspended: Suspicious Payment Activity"
  • Cross-referenced payment history — card had expired 6 days prior, auto-renewal failed silently
  • Reviewed Google's Suspicious Payment policy — confirmed billing integrity flag, not a content violation
  • Audited landing page and ad copy — no policy violations on content side
  • Documented full findings before taking any action
Action Taken
  • Updated payment method with client's new card details
  • Cleared outstanding balance in full before submitting appeal
  • Drafted structured appeal citing exact cause — expired card, not suspicious intent
"We have identified that the suspension was triggered by a payment method expiry resulting in a failed renewal. This was not indicative of suspicious activity. We have updated payment details and cleared all outstanding balances. We respectfully request reinstatement of the account."
Outcome
Result
Account reinstated within 36 hours of appeal submission
Lesson
Billing suspensions resolve faster when the appeal names the exact policy and eliminates intent concerns
Asset 02

Appeal Writing Sample

Case ADS-001 · Disapproval — Misleading or Exaggerated Claims Policy

Context
  • Ad flagged for headline containing "guaranteed results"
  • Client's landing page made no such guarantee — headline was the sole trigger
  • Fix applied: headline rewritten before appeal was submitted
Appeal Written
Subject: Appeal — Ad Disapproval / Misleading Claim

Thank you for reviewing this case. We have carefully reviewed the disapproval notice and identified that the flagged content was the ad headline, which contained the phrase "guaranteed results." We understand this falls under Google's Misleading or Exaggerated Claims policy, and we accept that this language was inappropriate.

We have updated the headline to accurately reflect our offering without making performance guarantees. The updated ad and landing page are now consistent with each other and fully compliant with Google's advertising policies.

We respectfully request that the updated ad be reviewed and approved. We are committed to maintaining policy compliance in all future campaigns. Thank you for your time.

Why This Works
  • Acknowledges the violation directly — no defensiveness
  • Names the specific policy — shows research was done
  • States the fix clearly — gives the reviewer confidence
  • Calm, professional tone — no frustration or urgency
  • Concise — respects the reviewer's time

Multi-Case Prioritisation

How I triage when three cases arrive simultaneously

The Scenario
  • Case A — Full account suspended (ads completely offline, revenue impact)
  • Case B — Single ad disapproved (rest of campaign running, low urgency)
  • Case C — Billing query (invoice discrepancy, no immediate ad impact)
Prioritisation Logic
Priority 1
Case A — Account Suspension
Full account offline = highest business impact. Investigate immediately, document findings, prepare appeal same day.
Priority 2
Case C — Billing Query
Billing issues escalate to suspension if unresolved. Address before it compounds.
Priority 3
Case B — Single Ad Disapproval
Campaign still running. Fix and appeal within 24h once A and C are stable.
Asset 05

Personal Appeals Framework

My standard operating process for every case — repeatable, documented, and improvable

01
Identify
Read the disapproval or suspension notice in full. Identify the exact policy cited. Do not assume or guess. Log the case immediately.
02
Investigate
Cross-reference the policy with the ad content, landing page, and account history. Determine whether the violation is in copy, URL, account behaviour, or billing.
03
Fix First
Fix the root cause before writing the appeal. Appealing without fixing is a wasted submission. Document what was changed and why.
04
Write the Appeal
Reference the specific policy by name. Describe the fix clearly. Calm, professional tone. Concise — appeals should be easy to act on.
05
Submit & Track
Submit through the correct channel. Log submission time in the case tracker. Set a follow-up reminder for 48–72 hours.
06
Follow Up
If unsuccessful, analyse the rejection. Escalate if needed. Adjust approach and resubmit with additional documentation.
